--- title: Subjects and time points description: Give a study's samples their subjects, time points and reference samples in ClonoLoom, and read how the Tracking view orders and checks them before any clonotype is followed. lede: Clonotypes are followed and tested within a subject, from one time to another. Before anything is tracked, ClonoLoom reads each sample's subject, time point and role from its annotation, orders each subject's samples in time, and says what it cannot place. ---

Annotate the samples

In the Data view's samples table, give each sample:

  • Subject: the person (or animal) the sample comes from. Every comparison over time is within a subject, and between groups the subject is the unit.
  • Time point: when the sample was taken, as days, weeks, months or years from the subject's reference event: an infusion, a transplant, a vaccination, the start of treatment (day 0).
  • Reference: for a subject's reference sample, what it is: infusion product, donor, baseline. Tracking can follow its clonotypes, and attribution measures each sample against it. A tissue such as infusion product, apheresis, graft or donor marks the sample as its subject's reference too.

Suggest from file names reads subjects and time points from the parts of the samples' names (P07_d28), and Fill from metadata from AIRR Repertoire metadata (subject_id, collection_time_point_relative with its unit and reference event). Scripts set them with annotate_samples (Remote control).

Samples at the same time (day 28 and week 4 are) are grouped as repeated samples within one subject. Their technical or biological relationship must be established from the study design; they do not add independent subjects. Only scientifically justified stable replicates should be used to estimate expansion variance. Cohort design checks require explicit handling of these repeated observations.

The Subjects part

The simulated CAR-T study, its subjects and time points read from its metadata.

Tracking › Subjects shows what ClonoLoom made of the annotation:

  • each subject's samples on one axis of days from its reference event, reference samples as diamonds, baseline samples in a column before the axis, replicates one above the other, and the subjects colored by condition; the plot saves as a figure;
  • a table of each subject's samples in time order, with their time points, references and replicates, and the samples left out;
  • the checks' findings, warnings first, each with its code.

Scripts read the same with describe_subjects. Then follow the subjects' clonotypes through their samples (Following clonotypes). Testing them between two samples, and attributing a sample to its reference, come with the next parts of the Tracking view.
